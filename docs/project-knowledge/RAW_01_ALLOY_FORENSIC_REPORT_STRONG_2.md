## Executive reconstruction and evidence standard

**Scope.** **[DOCUMENTED ALLOY STATEMENT / RESEARCH METHOD]** Alloy has operated a personal-training business since 1992, created a consulting/licensing platform in 2010, stopped selling new licenses as it shifted toward franchising in 2019, and has since expanded the branded studio model. The 2019 franchise-launch materials identified Matt Helland as VP of Programming and said he created Alloy’s programming at that point. The licensing system had previously been deployed in more than 1,000 facilities, while the franchise transition was explicitly intended to make locations conform more closely to the original “mothership” systems. citeturn25search0turn25search4turn25search9

## Programming philosophy, session architecture, and workout formats

**Programming philosophy (B).**

**[DOCUMENTED ALLOY STATEMENT]** Alloy’s older programming philosophy was unusually explicit. A historical Rick Mayo post prescribed three full-body strength workouts on alternate days, arguing that full-body sessions were more time efficient than four- or five-day splits and could distribute training volume across the week. It recommended grouping exercises into two-exercise supersets or three-to-four-exercise mini-circuits. The worked example was squat 3×6, push-up 3×10 and Pallof press 3×12. citeturn5view1

**[DOCUMENTED ALLOY STATEMENT]** That historical model also varied the loading character of the three weekly sessions: one day emphasized heavier weights/lower volume, another medium weights/medium volume, and a third lighter weights/higher volume/circuit work. Optional sprint/cardio sessions sat between the strength days, while weekends could be rest or active recovery. citeturn5view1

**[DOCUMENTED ALLOY STATEMENT]** Current Alloy language remains strongly strength oriented. Its functional-fitness material describes strength training as integral to functional fitness, emphasizing lifting/loading/bending/twisting/hauling/pulling/squatting and multi-joint movement across hips, knees, spine, shoulders, elbows and wrists. A 2026 corporate article describes squatting, hinging, pushing, pulling, carrying, stepping and rotating as patterns relevant to everyday function. citeturn16view0turn19search6

**[DOCUMENTED ALLOY STATEMENT]** A current Alloy location makes the template even more concise: two to three full-body strength sessions weekly built on squat, hinge, push, pull and carry, with challenging loads and progression inside a structure. citeturn21view0

**[REASONABLE INFERENCE]** Across old and new material, the underlying philosophy looks more stable than the delivery model: train movement patterns rather than bodybuilding body parts; use full-body sessions; prioritize useful strength; fit meaningful work into limited time; expose clients to unilateral and stability demands; and use conditioning without letting it displace strength as the principal adaptation. citeturn5view1turn16view0turn21view0turn19search6
